# Why the app teaches the way it does

Each choice below names the evidence behind it and how strong that evidence is for this use.
Where the app goes beyond what the research shows, it says so.

| Choice in the app | Evidence | Strength for this app |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Phrases come back for review just before they would be forgotten, each on its own schedule (FSRS-7, target recall 90 %). | Spacing beats massed study ([Cepeda et al., 2006](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.132.3.354)); recalling beats re-reading ([Rowland, 2014](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0037559)). FSRS predicts recall better than older schedulers on public review data ([srs-benchmark](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/srs-benchmark)). | Strong and general. |
| Exercises get harder as a phrase strengthens (recognise, then recall with help, then produce), aiming at about 85 % right answers. | Learning is fastest at roughly 85 % accuracy for a broad class of learning models ([Wilson et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-019-12552-4)). | Theoretical result for simple decision tasks. Applying it to phrase drills is our extrapolation. |
| A new phrase often opens with a guess before it is taught. | Answering before studying improves later memory when the answer follows ([Pan & Carpenter, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09814-5); meta-analysis: [St. Hilaire et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-023-02353-8)). | Good. |
| Listening drills rotate between the voices available on the device. | High-variability phonetic training, many speakers rather than one, improves L2 perception ([Uchihara, Karas & Thomson, 2025](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/studies-in-second-language-acquisition/article/high-variability-phonetic-training-hvpt-a-metaanalysis-of-l2-perceptual-training-studies/6ABB8C1F32D88D53EA8D05A4565E76F6), 79 studies). | Good for perception; the extra benefit of several voices over one is small. Depends on the voices installed. |
| Saying a phrase aloud, the learner sees which words the browser's speech recognition heard and which it missed, then can try again. | Recognition-based practice improves L2 pronunciation, with a medium overall effect (g = 0.69, 15 studies); feedback that points at the error helps, longer practice beats short, and the gain is large on sounds, small on stress and intonation ([Ngo, Chen & Lai, 2024](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0958344023000113), meta-analysis). Computer-assisted pronunciation training helps beginners and intermediate learners most ([Amrate & Tsai, 2024](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0958344024000181), systematic review). | Moderate: the studies are on learners of English. Dictation recognisers are built for native speech and lean on context, so they forgive some errors and miss others. |
| « Imagine » closes onboarding and every new trip with the learner's name, departure day and a scene matching their practice moment (the café, a terrace, the taxi or the station, the hotel booked in their name), over a photo of their destination. | Material tied to oneself is remembered better (self-reference effect, [Symons & Johnson, 1997](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.121.3.371), meta-analysis). In field experiments, the recipient's name in an email subject line raised opening by 20 % and sales leads by 31 % ([Sahni, Wheeler & Chintagunta, 2018](https://doi.org/10.1287/mksc.2017.1066)); a 2023 study did not reproduce it ([Defau & Zauner, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11002-023-09701-7)). | Plausible, mixed for the name alone; not measured in Bagage. |
| « Imagine » ends on the learner's plan: minutes a day, their moment, until the departure day; then the first lesson. | Induced positive fantasies about a desired future lowered the energy to pursue it, compared with neutral, negative or questioning ones ([Kappes & Oettingen, 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jesp.2011.02.003), four experiments); an if-then plan helps people act (row below). | The pairing is a design inference from both; not measured in this app. |
| A light game layer: XP, daily goal, streak, challenges, passport stamps, Bernard's journey. | Gamification has small positive effects on learning (g = 0.49), motivation (0.36) and behaviour (0.25); game fiction and competition combined with collaboration helped behaviour most ([Sailer & Homner, 2020](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-019-09498-w), meta-analysis). Rewards that inform rather than pay keep intrinsic motivation ([Deci, Koestner & Ryan, 1999](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.125.6.627)). | Modest and general; the motivational effect is the least stable. |
| The first daily goal starts a fifth done: the answers given during onboarding count towards it, and the first session ticket says so. | People given artificial progress towards a goal persisted more: 34 % of car-wash customers whose 10-stamp card already held 2 stamps completed it, against 19 % with an empty 8-stamp card; the reason given for the head start moderates the effect ([Nunes & Drèze, 2006](https://doi.org/10.1086/500480)). | A loyalty card, not learning: whether it carries over to a daily goal is not measured. |
| Near the daily goal, the rest is counted in phrases (« plus que 3 phrases »), and the session ticket offers a short session to finish. | Effort rises as a reward gets closer, in proportion to the distance left, for café reward cards as for online raters ([Kivetz, Urminsky & Zheng, 2006](https://doi.org/10.1509/jmkr.43.1.39)). | Same caveat; effort may also drop once the goal is reached. |
| Travellers sharing a phone play as a team: a weekly goal they reach together (five daily goals each) and who brought what, highest first, with a crown for the one ahead. | Among gamification studies, combining competition with collaboration had the strongest effect on behaviour ([Sailer & Homner, 2020](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-019-09498-w), meta-analysis). | Modest and general; the weekly target is our choice, not measured. |
| Mistakes never block a lesson; there are no hearts or energy. | Errors made while guessing or retrieving are part of learning (rows above). Duolingo itself dropped “one mistake costs a heart” as not the most effective way to support learning ([Duolingo, 2025](https://blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-energy/)). | Design judgement, consistent with the evidence. |
| The streak survives a missed day thanks to jokers, earned by practising and spent automatically. | Showing an intact streak increases the next engagement; a broken one lowers it, unless it can be repaired ([Silverman & Barasch, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1093/jcr/ucac029)). | Good, from consumer behaviour studies, not language learning. |
| Onboarding asks *when* you will practise (“with my morning coffee”). | Forming an if-then plan helps people act on their goals ([Gollwitzer & Sheeran, 2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0065-2601(06)38002-1), meta-analysis). | Good. Home brings the moment back at that time of day while the daily goal is open; push reminders are still to come (TODO.md). |
| The trip frames everything: destination, departure date, “imagine” screen, memory forecast for landing day. | Learners’ image of their future self using the language goes with more intended effort ([Al-Hoorie, 2018](https://doi.org/10.14746/ssllt.2018.8.4.2), meta-analysis); its link with actual achievement is weak. | Motivation only. The forecast itself is a direct readout of the memory model. |
| Bernard the mascot reacts to answers. | Animated pedagogical agents have a small positive effect on learning ([Castro-Alonso et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09587-1), meta-analysis). | Small effect; mostly for warmth. |
| Daily challenges, chest, passport stamps, XP. | Common game mechanics; no specific causal evidence that they improve learning. | Unproven. Kept light so they never replace review. |

## Not proven yet

- That this combination keeps people practising longer than other apps. It needs measurement
  (TODO.md, *Measure before claiming*).
- That pronunciation written “the French way” does not slow down accurate pronunciation in the long
  run. It helps on day one, which is the app’s promise; it can be turned off in *Moi*.
- The quality of translations from the translation service has not been evaluated on a test set.
- That the browser's recogniser judges French speakers fairly in the languages taught. Letting one
  word in four go unheard is a guess, to be tuned with the counts in TODO.md.
