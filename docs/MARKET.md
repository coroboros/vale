# What others ship (checked 18 November 2026)

What Duolingo, the other language apps and the tools a traveller already carries offer, and what
Bagage lacks. Facts first, each with its source; the last section is inference. Features change
quickly: check again before deciding.

## Language apps

| App | What matters for Bagage |
| --- | --- |
| Duolingo | Speaking is now central: the share of exercises answered by speaking doubled in 2026, and Video Call (conversation with an AI character) reaches every Super subscriber by the end of 2026 [1]. Listening exercises can be skipped for a lesson (« Can't listen now ») or switched off [2]. |
| Babbel | Babbel Speak (September 2025): AI voice conversations in guided real-life scenarios, still a beta [3][4]. |
| Speak | Audio-first role-plays and an AI tutor that corrects pronunciation as you speak [5]; AI Tutor Calls for Spanish and English since October 2026 [6]. |
| Pimsleur | Audio lessons to download and play offline, a hands-free mode [7]; role-plays checked by voice recognition and an AI Conversation Coach in beta for Latin American Spanish and French [13]. |

## Tools a traveller already has

| Tool | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Apple Live Translation | Translates the other person in AirPods, processed on the iPhone; Spanish, Italian and French included, in the EU since November 2025 [8]. Needs an iPhone 15 Pro or later and recent AirPods [9]. |
| Google Translate | Live speech-to-speech conversations in over 70 languages [10]; a practice mode with listening and speaking sessions, in beta for English speakers and for learners of English only [11]; pronunciation practice on Android [12]. |

## Bagage's gaps, most useful to a traveller first

1. **Feedback on pronunciation.** Shipped on 18 November 2026 where the browser has speech
   recognition: Bagage marks the words it heard and the ones it missed. Elsewhere, the learner
   compares their recording with the model and judges.
2. **Conversation practice with an AI.** Everyone above has it. Bagage has scripted scenes.
3. **Natural voices that work offline.** Bagage uses the phone's voices: offline, but robotic on
   some devices.
4. **Quiet mode.** Shipped on 18 November 2026: an hour without listening exercises or automatic
   sound, from a button in the exercise (Duolingo's « Can't listen now ») or the speaker in the
   session bar.

Plans for 2 and 3, and what is left of 1: TODO.md describes them, RUNBOOK.md says when.

## What this means (inference)

- Hearing a translation in the ear is free for many iPhone owners. « Translate for me » cannot be
  the product: Bagage's job is the confidence to speak yourself, before and during the trip, and
  to understand the reply.
- Speaking practice with feedback is expected in 2026, so it matters more than adding exercise
  types.
- Nobody above combines a trip date, a memory forecast for arrival day and offline phrases that
  work without an account. Those are worth keeping central.

## Where French travellers go (20 November 2026)

Asked: more places in the United States, Latin America for Spanish, Brazil for Portuguese, without
a list too long to choose from. The chips per language stay at nine at most, as for Spain.

- **The United States: California and Florida, next.** It is the first long-haul destination of
  the French, and « California, the U.S. National Parks, New York, and Florida remain the favorite
  destinations » ([US Commercial Service, France guide](https://www.trade.gov/knowledge-product/france-travel-and-tourism-tra)).
  1,589,676 French visitors in 2025, 7.3 % fewer than in 2024 ([L'Écho touristique, from NTTO's I-94 data](https://www.lechotouristique.com/article/etats-unis-100-000-touristes-francais-de-moins-en-2025)).
  Of all overseas visitors in 2024, New York had 9.8 million, Florida 8.9 million and California
  6.7 million ([NTTO, 2024 air travelers](https://www.trade.gov/feature-article/survey-international-air-travelers-results-2024)).
  Regions rather than cities, as for Andalusia: the French say « partir en Californie » for a road
  trip through its cities and parks, and Florida means Miami, Orlando and the Keys (our reading).
  Both are drawn, with their tips; their photos need the Mac (RUNBOOK A1). The tips' facts:
  Southern Californians put « the » before freeway numbers ([PBS SoCal](https://www.pbssocal.org/shows/lost-la/the-5-the-101-the-405-why-southern-californians-love-saying-the-before-freeway-numbers));
  75.3 % of Miami-Dade's residents speak a language other than English at home ([Census
  QuickFacts](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/fact/table/miamidadecountyflorida/POP815222)),
  mostly Spanish; Florida's tolls are mostly cashless, and a rental car pays them through the
  rental company ([SunPass](https://www.sunpass.com/en/tolls/rentalVehicles.shtml)).
- **Brazil: first in line, not yet.** 293,008 French visitors in 2025 (+24.6 %), Brazil's first
  European market ([TourMaG, from Embratur](https://www.tourmag.com/Le-Bresil-enregistre-une-hausse-de-20-des-touristes-europeens-en-un-an_a131347.html)).
  But Brazilian Portuguese has its own pronunciation and words, and teaching Lisbon's would train
  the ear on the wrong answers: it is a new language (TODO.md), and new languages wait for the
  decision figures (RUNBOOK C).
- **Latin America: after Brazil, as countries.** Spanish stays, but each country needs a pack of
  its words (ustedes, carro, jugo, celular) and prices in pesos or soles that « Prices by ear »
  cannot say yet: a coffee costs thousands of Colombian or Argentine pesos. The data, for the
  order: the region's most visited countries in 2025 were Mexico, the Dominican Republic, Brazil,
  Colombia, Chile, Argentina and Peru ([Dominican Today](https://dominicantoday.com/?p=253331));
  French visitors numbered 92,884 in Colombia ([Air Journal](https://www.air-journal.fr/?p=278818))
  and about 150,000 in the Dominican Republic ([L'Écho touristique](https://www.lechotouristique.com/article/republique-dominicaine-un-nouveau-record-pour-le-tourisme-en-2025)),
  mostly in all-inclusive resorts where Spanish matters less (our reading). No recent count of
  French visitors to Mexico was found.
